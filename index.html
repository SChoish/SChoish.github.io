I study how iterative computation and path generation shape policy improvement and long-horizon decision making. In multi-step proximal policy improvement, I analyze how the proximal step size τ changes the behavior of iterative refinement, using empirical sweeps and mathematical diagnostics to distinguish local, finite-step, and over-refinement regimes. In parallel, I am extending PathBridger toward PathFlower, exploring goal-conditioned state flows for long-horizon offline control.

Selected research

Manuscript 2026

PathBridger: Subgoal Bridges for Offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning

Soohyun Choi, Seonbin Cho, and Songnam Hong

PathBridger plans across long horizons by selecting an intermediate subgoal, constructing a plausible bridge in state space, decoding the bridge into short executable action chunks with inverse dynamics, and replanning during execution. The method is evaluated on OGBench navigation and manipulation tasks.

Ongoing research

Multi-step Proximal Policy Improvement

I study recursively re-centered proximal refinement after a base offline RL policy. My current focus is understanding the role of the step size τ: how tau sweeps, local approximations, and displacement diagnostics reveal qualitatively different refinement regimes. In particular, I examine when a step behaves like a local flow, when it enters a nonlocal finite-step regime, and when further refinement begins to over-optimize critic artifacts.

Current direction

PathFlower: Goal-Conditioned State Flows

PathFlower develops the next direction from PathBridger: moving from an explicitly selected subgoal and bridge toward final-goal-conditioned state flows that generate useful intermediate states without fixing a subgoal in advance. This is an ongoing research direction in generative planning and long-horizon offline goal-conditioned RL.
